# Clarify intent

Use an interview to uncover the outcome, users, constraints and evidence of success when those details would change the work. An explicit request to interview takes precedence over a shorter discovery process.

1. Inspect the request and available project context first. Separate known facts from assumptions; do not ask for information already available.
2. Identify the decisions blocked by uncertainty and the cost of a wrong assumption. Ask the most consequential question first, using concrete alternatives when helpful and allowing a free-form answer. Group closely related questions when that reduces effort.
3. Explore the user's actual problem, audience, current workflow, constraints and success criteria only as relevant. Do not impose a fixed number of questions or a numeric confidence score.
4. Summarize confirmed requirements, assumptions, unresolved questions and the next action. Confirm material or irreversible decisions when needed; otherwise proceed with reasonable stated assumptions and independent work.

If the user explicitly requests an interview, continue until the central goal and relevant constraints are understood or the user ends it. Do not portray an inference as a confirmed preference. Avoid treating elapsed time as agreement or converting the interview into permission for unrelated actions.

For a feature needing a written specification, use the available `workflow-spec-driven-development` skill when appropriate.
